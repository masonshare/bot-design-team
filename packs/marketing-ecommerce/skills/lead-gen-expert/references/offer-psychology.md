# Offer psychology notes

Load when scoring a concept, writing a landing page brief, or explaining why a choice works. These are thinking aids, not tricks. Every principle comes with an honest use and a line not to cross.

| Principle | What it says | Honest use in a lead magnet | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | A real favour creates goodwill | Give a genuinely useful asset with no hidden catch; the quick win is the favour | Hand over something thin and then pressure the reader |
| Commitment and consistency | Small yes answers lead to larger ones | Opt-in ladder: read, do the quick win, reply to a question, then consider the paid step | Trap people with pre-ticked boxes or hidden subscriptions |
| Scarcity | Limited things feel more valuable | State a limit only if it is real (event seats, a bonus with a true end date) | Fake countdowns, invented seat counts |
| Priming | What the reader sees just before frames the decision | Open the page with the problem the reader already has, so the magnet feels like the relief | Prime with fear unrelated to the product |
| Loss aversion | Losses feel larger than equal gains | Frame the cost of the problem staying unsolved ("the hours the manual process costs each week") using true numbers | Exaggerate harm or invent risks |
| Low-effort thinking | People take the path with the fewest decisions | Short form, one clear button, instant delivery, no jargon | Dark patterns that hide the unsubscribe or the price of the next step |
| Anchoring | A first number shapes how later numbers feel | Show the real value of the asset or the cost of the problem as context before the free price | Make up a "regular price" that was never charged |
| Zero-price effect | Free is more attractive than a small price | Use free for the first step; let the asset prove its worth so the next price feels fair | Call something free when it needs a card |
| Endowment | People value what they already have | After download, invite the reader to use it (a quick-win prompt, a reply request) so it becomes theirs | Nag unused downloads aggressively |

## Principle-to-format pairing

| Format | Principles that do most of the work |
|---|---|
| Checklist, cheat sheet | Low-effort thinking, reciprocity |
| Template, script | Reciprocity, endowment (they fill it in) |
| Quiz | Commitment (small answers), priming, identity |
| Calculator | Anchoring (a personal number), loss aversion (cost of inaction) |
| Email challenge | Commitment, reciprocity, endowment |

## Landing page pieces

- **Headline:** the outcome for one reader, in their words.
- **Bullets:** each names a specific thing they will have or know, with just enough curiosity to read on. Never promise more than the asset delivers.
- **Objections:** time ("takes about 10 minutes"), cost ("free, no card"), spam ("one email a week, one-click unsubscribe"), fit ("for X, not for Y").
- **Proof:** a real result or testimonial with permission; if none yet, show the method or sample page instead and say nothing invented.
- **Form:** the fewest fields that still let you follow up.

## Ethical check before shipping

1. Would the reader feel respected if they saw exactly how this was designed?
2. Is every claim, number and deadline true today?
3. Does the free asset stand on its own, with the paid step as a choice?
4. Is consent clear, and can they leave in one click?

If any answer is no, change the design.
