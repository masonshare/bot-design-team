# Funnel and list-building notes

Load when the user asks what happens after the opt-in, how to grow the list, how to design a quiz funnel, or how to wake a cold list. All numbers are reference points; check them against the user's own market and data.

## 1. Value ladder

| Rung | Purpose | Typical price |
|---|---|---|
| Free magnet | Prove you can solve a small problem | Free (email) |
| Low-cost step | Convert a reader into a buyer | Small, often a template, mini course or audit |
| Core offer | The main service or product | Mid |
| Premium | Done-with-you or high-touch | High |

Rule: the magnet addresses the problem the next rung solves. A magnet about invoicing that leads to a premium strategy retainer has a gap; add a middle step or change the magnet.

## 2. Hook, story, offer (inside each message)

- **Hook:** the first line earns the next ten seconds (a specific result, a surprising fact, a sharp question).
- **Story:** one real moment that shows the problem and the way out (invented in templates, true in live use).
- **Offer:** one clear action, either the day's task or the next step.

## 3. Follow-up sequence (5 to 6 messages)

| Message | Job |
|---|---|
| 0, immediately | Deliver the asset, say what comes next and how often, one-click start |
| 1, day 1 | The story of why this problem exists; second quick win |
| 2, day 2 | A common mistake and how to avoid it |
| 3, day 3 | Proof (a real case or the client's own result, with permission) |
| 4, day 4 to 5 | Soft introduction of the paid step: who it is for, who it is not for |
| 5, day 6 to 7 | Honest reminder and an easy way to ask a question |

After that, move the subscriber to a regular rhythm. Offer an easy unsubscribe in every message. Hand full copy to the email skill if installed.

## 4. Lead funnel types

- **Direct opt-in page:** one promise, one form. Best for warm or intent-driven traffic.
- **Survey or quiz entry:** the answers route the reader to a matching result and follow-up. Best when the audience has distinct situations.
- **Event or webinar registration:** the magnet is the session itself; works when the topic needs explanation.
- **Content upgrade:** a bonus inside one article; the best match between promise and reader intent.

## 5. Quiz funnel design

1. **Fit test.** Use a quiz if there are 3 to 5 distinct reader situations, each with a different next step, and enough traffic to justify setup. Otherwise a checklist is cheaper and just as good.
2. **Buckets first.** Name each result type, the next step it gets and the follow-up it triggers.
3. **Questions.** 5 to 10, easy first (identity or situation), one "what is your biggest frustration" free-text question near the end to harvest the reader's own words, one question that reveals readiness to act.
4. **Quiz page.** Promise what the reader will learn about themselves; state the time needed; no long form before the questions.
5. **Result page.** Aspirational type name, short description, one strength, one watch-out, one next step. Ask for the email before the result only if the page says so; otherwise show the result and then offer the full report by email.
6. **Segmented follow-up.** Each type gets its own first three messages, using the language collected in the free-text answers.
7. **Consent.** Tell people how the answers will be used.

## 6. Validation before building

- Ask 10 to 20 people from the target audience which of two or three titles they would sign up for.
- Or publish a one-line description and count real replies and clicks.
- Or look at which articles and posts already get saved and shared; turn the best into a magnet.
- Build the smallest version, ship it, then improve from the first 30 to 50 sign-up reactions.

## 7. Growth levers

| Lever | How |
|---|---|
| Content upgrade | One bonus per high-traffic article, matched to its exact promise |
| Specificity | "A 7-line script for late-payment reminders" beats "Business email tips" |
| Placement | Header bar, end of article, exit prompt that respects the reader, resource page, video description |
| Partnerships | Co-created magnet with a non-competing creator; both lists opt in on their own |
| Paid traffic | Only after the magnet converts organically; start with a small tested budget |
| Runway | Give the list time to warm up before a launch; start collecting early |

## 8. Reactivating a cold list

1. Segment: people who opened in the last 90 days versus the rest.
2. Send a short, plain, personal message to the rest: ask one easy question (for example, what is the one thing you are stuck on this month) from a monitored inbox.
3. Reply personally to every answer; the replies often become the next magnet topic.
4. After a clear last-chance notice, remove people who stay silent. A smaller engaged list sends better than a large dead one.

## 9. What to measure

Sign-up rate by source, delivery-email open rate, share who complete the quick win, share who take the next step, unsubscribe rate, spam complaints, revenue per subscriber after 60 to 90 days. If sign-ups are high and later steps are low, the promise is attracting the wrong people.
