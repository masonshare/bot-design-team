# Format templates (tool-neutral)

Skeletons for building a lead magnet. Fill the bracketed parts with audience-specific content. Delivery can be a PDF, a shared document, a spreadsheet, a form or quiz tool, or an email platform; the user picks. Every asset states its promise first, includes a quick win, shows a worked example with invented data, and ends with one natural next step.

## 1. Checklist (reader finishes in about 5 minutes)

```
# [Title]: [specific promise]
How to use: [1 to 2 sentences]

## [Area 1]
- [ ] [specific action, with the detail that makes it checkable]
## [Area 2]
- [ ] ...
## [Area 3]
- [ ] ...

## Start here (3 items with the biggest effect)
1. [ ] ...
## Next step
[one line that continues the help]
```
Notes: 9 to 15 items in 3 or 4 areas. Each item is an action ("check that the subject line names the benefit"), not advice ("improve emails"). The start-here block is mandatory.

## 2. Cheat sheet (one printed page)

```
# [Title]: [reference promise]
## [Topic 1]
| [Column] | [Column] | [How to apply] |
## [Topic 2]
| ... |
## Quick rules
When [situation], do [action].
## Insider tip
[one thing a basic search would not give]
## Next step
```
Notes: if it does not fit on one page, cut it. Conditional rules ("when X, do Y") are the core.

## 3. Fill-in template

```
# [Title]: [what they will have made]
How to use: [steps, under 10 minutes]
## Section 1: [name]
[Field]: [placeholder showing the format]
  Guidance: [what to write]
## Section 2: ...
## Completed example (invented data)
## Next step
```
Notes: 3 or 4 sections, 2 to 4 fields each, at most about 15 fields. Every field has a placeholder and a guidance line. The completed example is required.

## 4. Quiz or self-assessment

```
# [Title]: [what they will learn about themselves]
Questions: [5 to 10] | Time: [minutes] | Result types: [3 to 5]
## Question n: [text]  options A to D
## Scoring: pattern -> result type; tie-break rule
## Result: [type name]
Headline (identity, aspirational) | Description (2 to 3 sentences) | Strengths | Watch for | Next step for this type
## Implementation: tool choice; tag each person by result type for follow-up
```
Notes: design the result types first, then write questions that separate them. Each question should feel revealing, not tedious. Every result page links to a next step that fits that type. Say what happens to the answers (consent).

## 5. Calculator

```
# [Title]: [the number they will get]
Purpose: [1 to 2 sentences]
## Inputs: field | label | type | default | guidance
## Formula: [result] = ...; steps; worked example with invented numbers
## Output: main number; plain-language meaning; comparison bands
## Next step based on the result band
## Implementation: spreadsheet, form tool or small web page; where the email is asked
```
Notes: 3 to 5 inputs. Defaults let the reader press calculate at once. Comparison bands must come from the user's own data or be labelled as assumptions. Ask for the email before showing detailed results only if the page says so clearly.

## 6. Copy-and-paste script

```
# [Title]: [exact words for situation X]
When to use: [scenario]
## Opening > "[exact words]"
## Body > "[exact words, with breaks and variants]"
## Close > "[exact words]"
## Swap-in table: placeholder | replace with | example
## Variations: if [A], use [...]; if [B], skip [...]
## What not to say: 2 or 3 mistakes and why they fail
## Next step
```
Notes: it must sound like speech; read it aloud to test. The swap-in table is required.

## 7. Short email challenge (5 days)

```
# [Title]: [change in 5 days]
Overview: days | minutes per day | end result
## Day 1 to Day 5: theme, subject line, hook, lesson, action (under 10 minutes), quick win
## Day 1 full email (300 to 500 words)
## Settings: send time, personal sender name, monitored reply address
## Day 6 follow-up outline (soft introduction of the paid step)
```
Notes: each day must produce a visible result. The last day bridges to the paid offer without pressure. Full copy of the later emails goes to the email skill if installed.

## 8. Swipe file

```
# [Title]: [n examples of Y]
How to use: adapt, do not copy
## Swipe n: [name]  category | the example (invented) | why it works | adapt-it version with blanks
## Pattern table: pattern | short example | when to use
## Next step
```
Notes: at least 10 entries; the "why it works" and "adapt it" parts are what make it valuable. Use invented examples or the client's own material, never another company's copy.

## 9. Concept scorecard (offer architect output)

```
# Concept scorecard
Audience: [...] | Problem: [...] | Paid step: [...]
## Concept n: [title]
Format | one-sentence promise | time to value
Checklist scores: specific promise /5, quick win /5, low effort /5, natural next step /5, total /20 (one reason each)
Risks and assumptions
## Recommendation table: rank | concept | total | why it wins or when to pick it instead
Question: proceed with concept [x]?
```
Notes: three concepts minimum (quick win, insight tool, hybrid). Reasons must be specific to the concept.
