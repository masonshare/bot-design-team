# Creative library

Drafting aid. Every claim in a draft must be true, supportable and allowed by the brand profile. Examples use an invented brand, "Lumen Pantry" (a fictional pantry-organizer product).

## 1. Brief (one per ad set)

- Audience situation and awareness level
- Single promise (one sentence)
- Proof that exists (reviews, numbers, demos the owner can substantiate)
- Offer and call to action
- Placement, format, length limits
- Voice and banned claims (from the brand profile)

## 2. Angle prompts

| Angle | Question to answer | Invented example |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | What does the problem cost them today? | "Digging through a cluttered shelf every night?" |
| Desire | What does the good outcome look like? | "A pantry where every jar has a place." |
| Mechanism | Why does this work when others did not? | "Stackable bins sized to standard jars." |
| Proof | What evidence is there? | "Rated by 800 buyers" (only if true) |
| Comparison | Why this over the usual alternative? | "Bins versus loose baskets." |
| Objection | What stops people buying, and what answers it? | "Will it fit my shelf? Measure first with our guide." |
| Story | A person, a problem, a turning point | A short customer story with permission |
| Myth-busting | What common belief is wrong? | "More shelves will not fix clutter." |

Make 4 to 6 angles; give each 2 to 3 hooks; run one angle per ad.

## 3. Hook patterns (first line, first 2 seconds or first frame)

- Name the situation: "If your morning starts with a hunt for the coffee filters..."
- Ask a pointed question the audience answers in their head.
- Open with a concrete number or result.
- State a surprising fact, then explain it.
- Show the before state visually, no text needed.
- Address the person by role or situation, not by name.
- Start mid-action (a demo, a reveal) instead of an introduction.

A hook is tested on its own: same body, different first line.

## 4. Copy structures

| Structure | Use for | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| PAS | Problem Aware | Problem, make it vivid and true, solve |
| AIDA | Solution Aware | Attention, interest, desire, action |
| BAB | Transformation, story | Before, after, bridge (how) |
| Hook, story, offer | Video and creator-style ads | Stop the scroll, tell a short story, present the offer |
| Proof-led | Product Aware, Most Aware | Result or review, offer, deadline (only real), action |

Length follows awareness: more explanation for lower awareness, shorter for higher.

## 5. Format guide

| Format | Best use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short video (under 15 s) | Cold attention, demos | Hook in the first second; captions on; message works without sound |
| Longer video (30 to 90 s) | Story, education | Use after a viewer pool exists or for low-awareness audiences |
| Static image | Proof, retargeting, offers | One message; legible at small size |
| Carousel | Steps, before and after, feature walk-through | Each card must earn the swipe |
| Catalog or collection | Shoppers ready to browse | Warm and hot audiences |
| Search text | Intent capture | Keyword in headline, match query intent, vary headlines for testing |

Treat format as a test variable. Do not assume one format always wins.

## 6. Search ad text (responsive style)

Write many short headlines covering: the keyword, the main benefit, a proof point, an offer or price hint, a call to action, a differentiator. Pair with 3 to 4 descriptions that answer "why this, why now". Check platform character limits at the time of drafting; they change.

## 7. Creative checklist before handing over

1. One audience, one promise, one action.
2. Hook is specific and different from the previous batch.
3. At least one concrete detail (number, time, result) that is true.
4. Offer and next step are clear.
5. No banned claim; no invented testimonial or scarcity; no disguised ad format.
6. The landing page repeats the promise the ad made.
7. Variants differ in one element and are labelled with the hypothesis.

## 8. Creative diagnostic order (highest leverage first)

1. Offer: is it worth responding to? 2. Awareness match: is the ad pitched at the right level? 3. Sophistication: is the claim distinct from rivals? 4. Hook: does it stop the scroll? 5. Specificity: vague words replaced? 6. Giving versus asking: does it help before it sells? 7. Real reason to act now. 8. A single clear call to action. 9. Format fit. 10. Test hygiene: one variable, enough budget, enough time.

Most weak ads fail on 1 to 3, not on style.
