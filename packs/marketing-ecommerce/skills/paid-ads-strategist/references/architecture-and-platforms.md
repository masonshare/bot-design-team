# Architecture and platforms

Planning aid only. Nothing here is set up for the user. All numbers are reference points to check against the user's own market.

## 1. Audience tiers (traffic temperature)

| Tier | Who | Typical goal | Message depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Strangers matching a situation or interest | Earn attention, deliver an entry offer | Longer, explains the problem and the mechanism |
| Warm | Engaged with content, video viewers, site visitors who did not act | Answer objections, show proof | Medium, proof and comparison |
| Hot | Cart or form abandoners, trial users, past buyers | Close, upsell, win back | Short, direct, offer and deadline (only a real one) |

Build order for a new account: start with cold prospecting plus a small retargeting pool once enough visitors exist. Keep existing customers in their own campaign so they are excluded from prospecting.

Audience sources, from strongest signal to weakest: customer list and purchasers, high-intent site actions, engaged viewers or followers, lookalike or similar groups built from the strongest source, broad targeting with the creative doing the selecting, interest or topic targeting. With little data, broad targeting plus clear creative often beats narrow guesses; test it, do not assume it.

## 2. Funnel patterns by entry offer

| Entry offer | Fits | Ad destination |
|---|---|---|
| Direct purchase | Low price, simple, impulse or known need | Product or collection page |
| Lead magnet | Considered purchase, repeat nurture possible | Opt-in page |
| Trial or demo | Software or service with quick value | Sign-up page |
| Call or consultation | High price or complex sale | Short application page |
| Webinar or event | Education needed before buying | Registration page |

Rule of thumb as a reference point: the higher the price, the more steps between the ad and the sale. A cold ad that asks a stranger to pay a large sum usually fails; the fix is a smaller first step, not a louder ad.

## 3. Objective mapping

| Stage | Real outcome | Objective family to choose |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach, video views | Reach or video views; keep to a small share (reference point: 10 to 20% of budget) unless brand building is the declared goal |
| Consideration | Visits to content, engagement, leads | Traffic, engagement, lead forms |
| Conversion | Purchases, qualified leads, bookings | Sales or conversions, lead objective with a quality filter |

- Match the objective to the outcome the owner pays for. A click objective on a purchase goal attracts clickers.
- No conversion history: it may be necessary to start on a nearer event (add to cart, lead) for a learning phase with a stated end, then move to the real outcome once enough events exist (reference point: dozens of events per week, check the platform's current guidance).
- Lead objectives need a quality filter (a qualifying question, a price hint, a phone check) or lead volume will hide low quality.

## 4. Campaign structure pattern

```
Account
  Campaign A: Prospecting (cold), one objective
    Audience group 1: angle 1, angle 2, angle 3
    Audience group 2: same angles (to compare audience, not creative)
  Campaign B: Retargeting (warm and hot), excludes buyers
  Campaign C: Customers (upsell, repeat), optional
  Test lane: new angles or audiences, small fixed budget, separate from A and B
```

Rules:
1. Do not split a small budget across many groups; each group must be able to collect enough results to read. If funds are short, merge groups and test fewer things.
2. Avoid audience overlap between groups that bid against each other.
3. One naming scheme that encodes objective, audience, angle and date, so reporting is possible later.
4. Search campaigns: group keywords by intent, give each tight group its own matching ad text, review search terms regularly, add negative terms to remove irrelevant queries, and move converting terms into their own groups with tailored copy.
5. Keep a change log (date, change, reason). Changes made without a log make later diagnosis impossible.

## 5. Platform notes (flagged: features, formats, policies and costs change often; check current platform documentation before acting)

| Family | Strength | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Search ads | Captures existing demand, closest to purchase intent | Volume is capped by how many people search; keyword costs vary widely |
| Social feed and short video | Creates demand, large reach, creative-driven | Needs a steady supply of fresh creative; attribution can be fuzzy |
| Video platforms | Education and story-driven products | Longer production; view metrics are not outcomes |
| Professional network | B2B targeting by role, company, industry | High cost per click; use for high-value deals |
| Marketplace and shopping ads | Product feeds for shoppers already browsing | Feed quality and margin control matter |

Choosing: if people search for the product, begin with search. If the product needs explaining or nobody searches, begin with a social or video platform. B2B with a high deal value: consider the professional network despite higher cost. Use one or two platforms at first.

Regulated categories (health, finance, housing, employment, alcohol, and others) carry extra platform rules and legal limits. The user checks policy and law before launch; this skill does not give legal advice.

## 6. Measurement setup checklist

- Conversion events defined, firing once per real action, tested before launch.
- A source tag on every link so results can be split by campaign and ad.
- A blended view: total revenue over total marketing spend, next to platform-reported results.
- A check against real orders or qualified leads in the owner's own system.
- A review rhythm: daily glance for breakage, weekly decisions, monthly strategy.
- Note the attribution window used so reports are compared fairly.
