# Budget and decision rules

All figures below are **reference points** to be checked against the user's own market, margin, platform and tracking quality. Every spend number is a proposal for the owner to approve; the skill spends nothing.

## 1. Economics

```
Customer value (chosen window) = average order value x gross margin x expected orders in the window
Max cost per acquisition      = customer value x share willing to spend on acquisition
Monthly budget (proposal)     = target new customers x max cost per acquisition
```

Reference points for the share: one-time purchases usually aim to break even on the first order; subscriptions and repeat businesses aim for acquisition cost recovered within a few months; high-ticket services can accept a higher cost per acquisition because value is higher. A value-to-acquisition-cost ratio of about 3 to 1 is a common health reference point.

Invented worked example: Lumen Pantry sells a bin set at 40 with 50% margin, average 1.5 orders in the window, so value is 30. If the owner is willing to spend 60% of that, the max cost per acquisition is 18. Target 100 customers means a budget proposal of 1,800 for the month. Assumptions: margin and repeat rate are guesses until measured.

Platform-reported return can be inflated by attribution; compare with a blended ratio (total revenue over total marketing spend).

## 2. Metric chain

Impressions, cost per thousand, clicks, click rate, cost per click, conversion rate (clicks to results), cost per result, revenue, return on ad spend. Read left to right; a low cost per click is meaningless if conversion is near zero.

## 3. Allocation

| Model | Split (reference point) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| By maturity | 70 proven, 20 promising, 10 experiments | Running accounts |
| New account | Time-boxed learning phase with a stated end, most budget on one prospecting campaign and one retargeting pool | First 2 to 4 weeks |
| By funnel stage | Top 20 to 30, middle 30 to 40, bottom 30 to 50 | Adjust to brand maturity: new brands lean top, established brands lean bottom |
| By platform | Concentrate on 1 or 2 platforms until one is stable | Small budgets |

Never put 100% on proven campaigns; the experiment share finds the next winner. Small budgets (reference point: under a few thousand per month) should concentrate rather than spread.

## 4. Test budget

```
Minimum per variant = target cost per result x results needed to read
```

Reference points: ideally 30 to 50 results per variant per test window; with small budgets 10 to 20 results over a longer window, accepting lower confidence and saying so. Run at least 7 days to capture a weekly cycle; do not decide on fewer than 3 days. If a variant has spent about 3 times the target cost per result with zero results, it is a clear loser; close races run to the end.

Order of tests: offer, audience, hook, format, body copy, call to action. One variable per cycle.

## 5. Bidding progression (flagged: option names and rules change; check the platform)

| Data available | Choose | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| No results yet | Manual or lowest-cost with a cap you control | Algorithm has nothing to learn from |
| Some results (reference point: 15 to 30 per month) | Target cost per result | Stable campaigns |
| Strong data (reference point: 50 or more) | Target return or maximize conversions | Needs clean tracking |
| Rich data (reference point: 100 or more) | Maximize value | Variable order values |

Rules: do not start a new campaign on the most automated strategy; set a target 10 to 20% looser than the real limit so delivery is not choked; expect a 1 to 2 week learning dip after a change and do not flip back in panic; if the daily budget is under about 5 times the target cost per result, the campaign may under-deliver.

## 6. Cut, hold, scale (set these before launch)

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Fewer results than the minimum data, under 3 days | **Hold**. Say "not enough data" |
| Spent about 3x target cost per result, zero results | **Cut** the variant |
| Cost per result above target by more than about 30% after the minimum data, and trend is flat or worse | **Cut** or fix (check chain) |
| Within about 15% of target, trend stable | **Hold**, keep testing creative |
| At or better than target for at least 5 to 7 days with enough results | **Scale** by 10 to 20% per step, spaced 3 to 5 days |
| After a scaling step cost per result rises by more than about 15 to 20% | **Hold or step back**, then scale horizontally |
| Frequency high and click rate falling | **Refresh** creative, do not raise budget |
| Tracking or approval problem | **Pause the decision**, fix measurement first |

The percentages are starting points. Write the user's own numbers into the plan before launch, not after.

## 7. Scaling paths

- **Vertical**: raise budget on a winner in small, spaced steps. Duplicating a winner into many copies can make them bid against each other.
- **Horizontal**: new audiences, new angles on the same audience, new formats, new platforms, new regions. Test with the experiment share, not by moving proven money. Platform migration needs adapted creative, not a copy-paste.
- Track marginal cost per result (cost of the extra spend) as well as the blended average.
- Scale what wins. Adding budget to weak segments only multiplies losses.

## 8. Fatigue signals and refresh

| Signal | Reference point |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Above about 3 for cold audiences; above about 6 for warm |
| Click rate | Down more than about 20% over two weeks |
| Cost per result | Up more than about 15 to 20% over two weeks with no other change |
| Cost per thousand | Up more than about 25% without a competitive reason |
| Negative feedback | Rising |

Refresh ladder: new hook on the same ad, then new visual treatment, then a new concept, then bring back a rested winner after a few weeks. Keep the next batch ready before the current one fades. Retargeting pools tire faster because they are small.

## 9. Reporting honesty

Report platform and blended results side by side; flag gaps between platform-reported and real sales; do not hide a weak blended result behind a strong platform number; label estimates as estimates.
