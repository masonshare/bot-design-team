# Copy frameworks and psychology (condensed)

Use when SKILL.md is not enough: picking a structure, diagnosing a market, choosing persuasion levers, or writing B2B and video scripts. All examples are invented.

## 1. Structures

| Name | Order | Best for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIDA | Attention, interest, desire, action | Ads, short emails, posts | Interest step often skipped; desire needs proof |
| PAS | Problem, agitate, solution | Problem-aware readers | Agitation must stay honest; do not invent fear |
| Story-based | Hero (reader) has a problem, meets a guide (you), gets a plan, sees stakes, reaches success | About pages, brand pages | The reader, not the founder, is the hero |
| 4Ps-style | Promise, picture, proof, push | Long sales pages | Picture = show life after, specifically |
| Feature to benefit to meaning | What it is, what it does, what it lets you do | Product descriptions | Stop at the level the reader cares about |
| Motivating sequence | Attention, need, satisfaction, visualisation, action | B2B pitches, letters | Satisfaction must tie to the stated need |
| Before, after, bridge | Today, desired state, how to get there | Short-form, social | Make the bridge concrete |

If two fit, pick the one matching the awareness level in SKILL.md.

## 2. Market diagnosis

**Sophistication stages** (how many similar promises the reader has already heard):
1. New category: a simple direct claim works.
2. Rivals copy the claim: make it bigger or faster, with proof.
3. Everyone has big claims: explain the mechanism, the how.
4. Mechanisms copied: improve or specify the mechanism; name it only if it really is distinct.
5. Market is tired: lead with identity, belonging or the experience; claims matter less.

**Desire channelling.** Copy rarely creates a wish; it connects an existing wish (more time, less worry, status, belonging, safety) to the offer. Name the wish, then show the route.

**Demand check.** Strong copy cannot rescue an offer nobody wants. If audience, offer and channel do not match, fix those before the words.

**Message-audience-channel.** One message for one audience through one channel. A mismatch often shows as clicks without action.

**Personal versus promotional feel.** Pieces that read like a note to one person tend to get read; pieces that look like standard promotion get skipped. Use a human sender, a specific opening and plain layout where the channel allows.

**Claim discipline.** A claim nobody else makes, if true and provable, can be your headline. Every claim carries a reason ("because...") or proof next to it.

## 3. Influence principles (use honestly)

| Principle | In copy | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give a real tip, sample or tool first | A "free" item with hidden cost |
| Commitment | Ask a small step first (quiz, trial), then the larger one | Trick enrolments |
| Social proof | Count of real customers, real reviews, similar people | Fake or unverifiable numbers |
| Authority | Verifiable credentials, experience, press with permission | Invented titles |
| Liking | Warmth, shared values, a human voice | Fake intimacy |
| Scarcity | Real limits and the reason for them | Countdown timers that reset |
| Unity | "People like us" identity | Excluding groups unfairly |

Decision shortcuts that help: a loss is felt more than an equal gain (state what staying put costs, truthfully); the first number seen anchors judgment (show the full value or the comparison before the price); framing changes feel ("saves 30 minutes" versus "stops wasting 30 minutes"); fast intuitive judgment comes first, so layout and first line matter before the argument. Keep the reader moving: each sentence's job is to get the next one read (curiosity, a promise of detail, a short transition).

## 4. Voice-of-customer method

1. Collect raw sentences: reviews (yours and rivals'), support messages, interview notes, forum posts.
2. Tag each as pain, desire, objection, trigger event or alternative tried.
3. Pick repeated phrases; keep them near-verbatim for headlines and bullets.
4. Ask in interviews: what happened right before you looked, what else did you try, what almost stopped you, what changed after.
5. Never present an invented sentence as a quote.

## 5. Offer building

A strong offer is a clear promise, a defined outcome, a fair price, real extras and removed risk. Value ideas: speed, ease, certainty, support, bonuses that solve the next problem, a guarantee the client truly stands behind (the client provides terms). Ladder offers from free to small paid to core so each step earns the next; do not pad the stack with filler bonuses.

## 6. B2B notes

- Several readers judge at once: users want ease, finance wants cost and payback, leaders want risk and strategy, technical staff want specifics. Give each a short section.
- Lead with business outcome, support with feature detail, close with a low-risk next step (call, pilot, audit).
- Proof beats adjectives: a case with before, after, time frame (only real ones; otherwise placeholder).
- Long copy is fine when the reader is evaluating; each section should answer one question they have.
- Steady, clear style: short sentences, concrete nouns, no jargon the reader would not use.

## 7. Video and webinar scripts

**Short VSL (3 to 6 minutes):** hook with the reader's problem, a quick story of why this matters, the solution and how it works, proof, offer with price, guarantee, one close.
**Long VSL or webinar (15 to 60 minutes):** opening promise, who you are and why you can say this, a teaching section that changes one false belief, a bridge story (the moment of insight), the offer stack, objection answers, a deadline only if real, Q&A.
Rules: one belief shift per segment, one offer, spoken rhythm, on-screen text repeats key numbers, mark `[PROOF NEEDED]` slots, give a timecode estimate.

## 8. Platform notes

- Feed posts: first line is the hook (it is truncated); one idea; end with a question or one step; hashtags few and relevant.
- Professional networks: concrete lesson or result, plain first-person voice, short paragraphs.
- Image-led networks: the caption supports the visual; benefit first, line breaks, one action.
- Email: subject line and preview text work as a pair; one idea; one link as the main action.
- Ads: respect character limits and each platform's policy; check current specs before finalising, because they change.

## 9. Testing

Change one element per test (headline, offer, proof, CTA, length). Define the metric before launch and the sample you will wait for. Small traffic cannot detect small gains; prefer bold differences. Record the result as a new baseline.
