# Quality signals checklist (experience, expertise, authority, trust)

Use it to specify requirements in a brief and to review a page. Signals must be real and checkable; never invent credentials, reviews or sources.

## Experience
- First-hand proof: original photos, screenshots, test results, a documented process, a dated account of using the thing.
- A reason the author is in a position to know (they did it, they run it, they measured it).
- Details only a person who did it would mention: failures, costs, surprises.

## Expertise
- Named author with a real bio and relevant background; a named reviewer for sensitive topics.
- Depth beyond the first page of competitors: edge cases, trade-offs, when the advice does not apply.
- Accurate terms and correct claims; facts checked against primary sources.

## Authority
- The site and author are cited or recommended by others in the field.
- Primary sources linked (studies, official documents, datasets), not other summaries.
- Original data or a unique tool that others reference.

## Trust
- Clear who runs the site: contact details, about page, policies, a complaint or return route.
- Honest disclosure of commercial relations, sponsorship and affiliate links.
- Dates, update notes, a corrections policy; secure site; no deceptive layout.
- Claims match evidence; no inflated promises.

## High-stakes topics (health, money, safety, legal, major life decisions)
Require a qualified author or reviewer named on the page, cited primary sources, a visible update date, and cautious wording. If the business cannot provide a qualified reviewer, recommend not targeting the topic.

## Myths to avoid
- These signals are not a single score you can switch on with a plugin or a tag.
- An author box alone does not create expertise.
- AI-written text is judged on usefulness, accuracy and originality, not on who or what typed it. Require human review, first-hand input and fact checks.

## Quality review in five questions
1. Would a knowledgeable reader trust this page with their own decision?
2. Does it say something the top results do not?
3. Can each key claim be traced to a source or an experience?
4. Is it clear who wrote it and why they are qualified?
5. Is the main purpose to help the reader, not to catch a query?

## Share of weak pages
If a large share of the site is thin, duplicated or made only for search, it can drag down the whole site. Before adding pages, prune or fix the weak ones; recovery usually takes months and follows real improvement, not cosmetic edits.
