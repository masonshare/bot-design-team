# Copywriter handoff spec (per email)

Use `copywriter-skill` if installed and paste the block as its brief. If not installed, use the block to draft the email in place. The block is plain text any writing agent can read; nothing here sends anything.

## How to use

1. Fill every field from the approved Journey Blueprint and the client's brand profile. Mark unknowns as "to confirm", never guess.
2. One block per email. For a batch, number the blocks and add a summary table on top (email number, day, subject angle, trigger, call to action).
3. Ask the writer to return: 3 subject lines, 2 preview texts, the body, and a one-line note on which trigger each paragraph carries.

## Block

```
EMAIL BRIEF (draft only, not for sending)

Campaign: <name>            Email: <n of N>           Journey type: <welcome | cart | win-back | ...>
Position in arc: <what the reader already knows, what happened in the previous email>
Reader state: <awareness, mood, last action, segment>
Segment rule: <who receives this, who is suppressed>

Voice directive: <paste voice or tone chart from brand profile; if none, "plain, warm, direct">
Language and reading level: <language, target reading level>

Mission: <one sentence, what this email must achieve>
One big idea: <one sentence>
Opening hook type: <question | story | observation | direct statement>

Subject line angle: <direction, not final copy>
  Example phrasings: <2 or 3, labelled by pattern>
Preview text direction: <what it adds to the subject>

Dominant trigger: <trigger> - how to apply: <one line>
Supporting trigger (optional): <trigger> - how: <one line>
Trigger limits: <what must be true for scarcity or urgency; if not true, omit>

Call to action: <single action, button text direction, destination, placement>
Length range: <words>
Proof to include: <testimonial, number, case; mark source and permission>
Offer details: <what, price band, real deadline if any, guarantee wording>

Must include: <unsubscribe, sender identity, address or other legal footer per local rules (user to confirm), links, alt text>
Forbidden: <fake urgency, false claims, medical or income promises, competitor attacks, the brand's banned words, filler like "just checking in">
Style bans: <list from brand profile>

Success metric: <click, reply, purchase>    Test planned: <variable, or none>
Return: 3 subject lines, 2 preview texts, body, trigger note per paragraph.
```

## Example (invented)

```
EMAIL BRIEF (draft only, not for sending)

Campaign: Cedar & Co welcome        Email: 4 of 6        Journey type: welcome
Position in arc: Reader got the guide on day 0, read the founder story on day 1, tried the brewing tip on day 3.
Reader state: Curious, has not bought. Segment: signed up from the guide page.
Segment rule: Send if no order; suppress buyers.

Voice directive: plain, warm, a little dry humour, short sentences.
Language and reading level: English, grade 7.

Mission: Show that real customers like the starter box, remove one doubt.
One big idea: People who were unsure about loose-leaf tea found the starter box easy.
Opening hook type: short customer story.

Subject line angle: a customer's doubt turned around.
  Example phrasings: "I thought loose tea was fussy" (personal note); "Three people, one starter box" (number).
Preview text direction: add the surprising part of the story.

Dominant trigger: social proof - how: one named, permissioned review with a specific result.
Supporting trigger: authority - how: one line on how the box is chosen.
Trigger limits: no scarcity in this email.

Call to action: See the starter box (single button, after the review).
Length range: 150 to 220 words.
Proof to include: two reviews, source and permission to be confirmed by the client.
Offer details: starter box, mid price band, no deadline.

Must include: unsubscribe link, sender name, postal address as required locally (client confirms).
Forbidden: fake stock counts, health claims, "limited time".
Style bans: per brand profile.

Success metric: click to product page.    Test planned: subject, personal note vs number.
```
