# Psychological trigger table (ethical use)

Triggers explain why a message works; they are not tricks. Use them to make a true benefit easier to see, never to hide a cost or invent pressure. All placements below are reference points.

## Rules of use

1. At least one trigger per email, at most three, one dominant.
2. Trust before pressure: early emails use give-and-relate triggers, late emails may use time-based ones, and only when real.
3. Scarcity, urgency and loss framing need a real, checkable fact (stock, seats, a dated deadline). If you cannot state the fact in one line, drop the trigger.
4. Do not combine pressure with generosity in one email (it reads as bait).
5. Always leave an easy way to say no or unsubscribe.
6. Never use health fears, money fears or personal vulnerability as levers.

## Trigger reference

| Trigger | Plain meaning | Where it works best | Ethical application | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give first, people feel inclined to return value | Early emails, body | A real useful guide, tool or tip, no strings | Gift followed by hard pressure |
| Commitment and consistency | Small yeses lead to larger ones | Middle, call to action | Ask for a tiny step (pick a topic, reply, click) | Tricking people into a larger commitment |
| Social proof | Others like me chose this | After a claim, before the call to action | Specific, real, permissioned testimonials; counts you can verify | Vague "thousands love us", fabricated reviews |
| Authority | A credible source reduces doubt | Early to middle | Real credentials, results, process, honest limits | Inflated titles, borrowed logos |
| Liking | We respond to people we know and relate to | Throughout, voice and story | Consistent sender name, honest personality | Faking closeness or fake first-name familiarity |
| Unity | Shared identity or community | Middle to late | Name the real group and what it values | Us-versus-them hostility |
| Scarcity | Limited supply or places | Last 2 to 3 emails | State the exact limit and where it comes from | Fake "only 3 left" |
| Urgency | A dated deadline | Last 1 to 2 emails | A real close date and time zone | Evergreen "ends tonight" that never ends |
| Loss aversion | Losses feel larger than equal gains | Final stage, after trust | Describe a genuine cost of waiting (price changes, missed season) | Fear, shame, catastrophe framing |
| Curiosity gap | A gap in knowledge creates a pull to resolve it | Subject line, opening | Open a loop the email closes within it | Clickbait with no payoff |
| Identity shift | People act in line with who they see themselves becoming | Middle | Show the "after" self in plain, respectful terms | Telling readers they are failures now |
| Future pacing | Picturing the result makes it concrete | 1 to 2 emails before the offer | Describe a realistic day with the result, then bridge to how | Unrealistic promises, guarantees you cannot back |
| Pattern interrupt | Break a routine to regain attention | When opens decline | A short, plain, personal message in a different format | Using it when the pattern works, shock tactics |

## Stacking guide

| Dominant | Pairs well with | Avoid pairing with |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Authority, future pacing | Urgency |
| Reciprocity | Liking, commitment | Scarcity, loss aversion |
| Social proof | Authority, identity shift | Pattern interrupt |
| Authority | Social proof, future pacing | Loss aversion |
| Scarcity | Urgency, loss aversion (all true) | Reciprocity |
| Identity shift | Unity, future pacing | n/a |

## Typical arc across a 7-email journey

Emails 1 to 2: reciprocity, curiosity, liking. Emails 3 to 4: authority, social proof. Emails 5 to 6: identity, future pacing, commitment. Email 7: scarcity, urgency, loss aversion, only if real.

## Common deployment errors

- Scarcity in the second email of a first-contact journey.
- Authority with no warmth.
- A vision ("imagine...") with no bridge to how to get there.
- Loss framing in nurture before the reader has decided they want the thing.
- Interrupting a pattern that already performs.
- New free content right after purchase that makes the buyer feel they overpaid.
- Proof without specifics (who, what changed, how long).
