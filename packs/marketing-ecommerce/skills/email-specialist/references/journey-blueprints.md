# Journey blueprints (starting points)

All timings and counts are reference points. Adapt to the offer, the audience, and the intensity setting. Invented example brand used throughout: "Cedar & Co", a fictional tea shop.

## Common rules for every journey

- Entry trigger, exit condition, and buyer suppression are written before the first email.
- One idea and one main call to action per email.
- Every journey names where people go next (routing).
- Transactional messages (order confirmation, receipt, shipping notice) are not marketing; keep their content clear and do not hide promotions inside them. Check local rules on what counts as which.
- Include sender identity, a working unsubscribe, and the legally needed footer; the user confirms local requirements.

## 1. Welcome (new subscriber, 4 to 6 emails over 7 to 10 days)

Goal: deliver what was promised, build trust, earn the first purchase or reply.

| Day | Job | Dominant trigger | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Deliver the promised item, set expectations (what and how often) | Reciprocity | Open the item |
| 1 | Who we are and why, a short true story | Liking | Reply with one question |
| 2 to 3 | Best-known idea or quick win | Authority | Read or try it |
| 4 to 5 | Proof: real customer result or review | Social proof | See the product |
| 6 to 7 | First offer, with a real small incentive if one exists | Future pacing, soft deadline only if real | Buy or book |
| 9 to 10 | Last reminder or "not for you yet? choose your topics" | Commitment | Pick preferences |

Branch: clicked the offer but did not buy, goes to a cart-style reminder; bought, goes to post-purchase. Non-buyers move to nurture at day 10.

## 2. Nurture (ongoing, 1 to 2 per week)

Goal: stay useful so the next offer lands on warm ground. Rotate content types: story, how-to, proof, curation, behind the scenes, offer. Keep a value-to-sales balance (see the main skill). Tier by engagement; cold people move to win-back.

## 3. Cart recovery (abandoned cart or checkout, 3 emails over 3 to 4 days)

Goal: help someone finish a purchase they already wanted.

| Delay | Job | Dominant trigger | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 hour | Reminder with the items, one clear button | Commitment | Plain, helpful, no pressure |
| 24 hours | Answer the likely doubts: sizing, delivery, returns, reviews | Social proof | Add support contact |
| 48 to 72 hours | Last note; incentive only if the business genuinely allows it | Loss aversion (true cost only) | State any real deadline; stop after this |

Suppress as soon as an order is placed. Do not keep mailing people who bought through another path. If a discount is used, consider testing a no-discount version first to avoid training buyers to abandon carts.

## 4. Win-back (lapsed customers or subscribers, 2 to 4 emails over 2 to 3 weeks)

Goal: find out who still wants to hear from you, and let the rest go gracefully.

| Step | Job | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal, short "still interested?" with one clear choice | Pattern interrupt, curiosity |
| 2 | What is new or what they missed, one useful item | Reciprocity |
| 3 | A real offer or a preference choice (less often, other topics) | Commitment |
| 4 | Final notice: "we will stop sending unless you click" | Honest consequence |

After the final notice, stop mailing non-responders (sunset). This protects reputation more than any extra send.

## 5. Post-purchase (3 to 5 emails over 2 to 4 weeks)

Goal: reduce regret, help the customer succeed, earn reviews and repeat orders.

| Timing | Job | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Right after order | Confirm, set delivery expectations | Reassurance (transactional) |
| After delivery | How to use or get the most from it | Authority, reciprocity |
| 7 to 14 days | Ask for feedback or a review, one click | Commitment, unity |
| 21 to 30 days | Related item or refill reminder, timed to real usage | Future pacing |
| Later | Join the loyalty or referral option | Unity |

Rule: do not introduce free value that makes the purchase feel like a mistake; reinforce the decision.

## 6. Launch (a defined offer with a real open and close, 8 to 12 emails over 2 to 3 weeks)

Phases: anticipation (useful content, three emails), open (offer, two to three emails), objection handling (proof and answers), final stretch (the last 48 hours, with an honest deadline). Only assertive intensity adds a second email on the final day. Non-buyers return to nurture after close; cool down for a few days before the next promotion.

## 7. Webinar or event follow-up

Before: confirmation, reminder a day ahead, reminder an hour ahead. After: replay to attendees and no-shows with different first lines, then offer, then proof, then close. Attendees and no-shows get different branches. Non-buyers go to nurture.

## Routing map

| From | Buyer goes to | Non-buyer goes to | Silent goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Post-purchase | Nurture | Win-back |
| Cart recovery | Post-purchase | Welcome-style nurture | Sunset review |
| Launch | Post-purchase | Nurture, cool-down first | Win-back |
| Win-back | Nurture | Sunset | Sunset |
| Post-purchase | Nurture or loyalty | n/a | Win-back later |
