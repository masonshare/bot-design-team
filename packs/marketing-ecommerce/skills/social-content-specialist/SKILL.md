# Social Content Specialist

## What it does

It turns a business goal into a content system and a set of drafts: pillars, a calendar, platform-native posts with strong openings, a plan to multiply one idea into many pieces, and a diagnosis when a post underperforms. It produces drafts and plans only. It never posts, schedules, replies to real users or spends money.

If the client keeps a `brand-profile.md` (name, positioning, audience, tone, products, limits), read it first and do not re-ask what it answers. If a voice handoff block exists (from a voice or brand skill), apply it to tone and vocabulary; this skill's frameworks govern structure and strategy, the voice governs character. If neither exists, ask once: who the audience is, what the account sells or stands for, and the tone wanted. Offer to write any new voice or pillar decisions back as a new draft section, never overwriting the file.

## Plan: pillars, calendar, cadence

Intake (guided): platforms in use or considered; what is sold or promoted; audience and its main struggle; existing long-form material (newsletter, podcast, video, blog); hours per week available for content. The hours decide cadence, so never skip it.

Steps:
1. **Angle.** Find the intersection of what the client knows deeply and what the audience wants, then narrow to a slice few others own (Content Tilt). Test: if all the account's posts vanished, would anyone miss them? If not, narrow further.
2. **Mission line.** One sentence: "[Brand] is where [specific audience] gets [type of content] that helps them [concrete benefit]". Use it as a filter: an idea that does not fit is cut.
3. **Pillars.** Pick 3 to 5 pillars (recurring themes). For idea volume, cross sub-topics (8 to 12 at most) with delivery styles (6 to 8 the client can execute: story, tip, list, contrarian take, before-and-after, how-to, question). The grid produces candidates; the client picks winners.
4. **Mix.** Plan value posts (teach, entertain, show behind the scenes) against asks (buy, sign up, book). Reference point: at least 3 value posts per ask, more for a young account. A post cannot be both: a buried call to action does not make a post valuable.
5. **Cadence.** Pick a rhythm the client can sustain for 90 days; consistency beats bursts. Match formats to platforms with `references/repurposing-map.md`. Platform behaviour changes fast, see the platform section.
6. **Calendar.** Fill `assets/content-calendar-template.md` with specific assignments (day, platform, format, topic, opening line, value or ask).
7. **Series triggers.** Tie some recurring slots to a recurring cue (a weekday, a season, a habit) so the audience learns when to expect you.

Output: a Content Strategy Brief (angle, mission line, pillars, platforms, one-week or four-week calendar, mix check, measurement plan).

## Draft: platform-native posts

Intake (guided): platform; core message; the one reader and what they should feel, think or do after; voice (from the brand profile or handoff).

Steps:
1. **Hook first.** Write 5 to 10 candidate openings with `references/hook-patterns.md`, pick the one that would stop a scroll on that platform. The opening must connect honestly to the content that follows.
2. **Arc.** Hook, then story or evidence that keeps paying off the hook, then a takeaway with one clear next step. Text posts: hook is the first line before the "see more" cut; closing line carries the call to action or question.
3. **Share test.** Does it give the reader social currency (they look smart passing it on), practical value (worth saving), a high-energy emotion (awe, amusement, justified frustration, not mild contentment) or a story? Hit at least two.
4. **Reader test.** Score usefulness, inspiration and empathy 1 to 5 each. Any score under 3 means revise; if one is zero the post is empty.
5. **Write natively** for the platform: length, line breaks, tone, call-to-action style. Never paste the same text everywhere.
6. **Offer one variant** (different hook angle, different format or another platform).

Output: the draft, a one-line note on which hook pattern and tests were applied, and a revision offer. Mark any invented fact, number or testimonial as a placeholder to be replaced with a real one; never fabricate proof.

## Repurpose: one idea, many formats

Use `references/repurposing-map.md`. Core method:
1. Identify the source (video, podcast, newsletter, blog, talk) and pick the extraction path.
2. Mark 6 to 12 standalone moments (thesis, story, surprising point, step, contrarian claim, data point). Do not chop chronologically.
3. Score each moment: can it stand alone without the source, and would it stop a scroll? Drop the ones that fail.
4. Rebuild each kept moment for its destination (new hook, new example or angle, native format), not a resized copy. Test: a follower who sees two of the pieces should feel they are independent content on the same topic.
5. Space the pieces across several weeks, strongest first, ask last.
6. Return a source-to-post map so the client sees what came from where.

Cheap idea test before big production: publish the core idea as a very short post first and expand only what earns clearly above-average response.

### Always show one worked example
When planning a month, include one idea fully repurposed into at least three different formats (for example a short video, a carousel, a text post, a story), each with its own hook and cut, so the user sees the method and not just the cadence.

## Diagnose: why a post underperformed

Intake: the post, the platform, the metrics (reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, watch time), what was expected, and whether it is a one-off or a pattern.

Steps:
1. **Rate three parts** from 1 to 5: opening, body (retention, structure), takeaway (clear next step).
2. **Match the symptom:** good reach but no shares points to low-energy emotion or no social currency; saves but no shares points to practical value without a story; low reach on a good post points to a weak opening or wrong format; low reach everywhere points to cadence, angle or audience mismatch.
3. **Compare** with the account's own best and worst posts in the same format to isolate what differs (opening, pacing, length, visual).
4. **Check fit** with the platform (format, timing, consistency) using the flagged guidance below.
5. **Name the primary failure point** (usually the opening) and give a concrete rewrite. If it is a pattern, recommend a structural change: angle, mix, format or cadence.

Output: a diagnosis with at least one quantified score, the main failure, a rewrite, and the one thing to test next.

## Community management basics

- Reply to comments in the first hour when possible, with a specific response, not a generic thanks; questions deserve an answer or a clear "we will check".
- Draft replies in the brand voice but never send them; the user posts.
- Triage: praise (thank and amplify), question (answer, consider turning into content), complaint (acknowledge, move detail to a private channel, never argue publicly), abuse or spam (follow the account's moderation policy, escalate legal or safety issues to a human).
- Never fake engagement: no bought followers, invented comments or staged testimonials.
- Ask permission before reposting a customer's content or message.
- Turn repeated questions and objections into the next pillar topics.

## Platform adaptation (reference points, verify before relying on them)

Platform rules, formats and algorithms change fast and differ by account. Treat everything here as a starting hypothesis and check the platform's current help pages and the account's own analytics.
- **Short-vertical video platforms:** the first seconds decide everything; strip preamble; completion and rewatch usually matter more than likes.
- **Professional network:** personal narrative and opinion in text or document carousels tend to do well; the first lines before the fold are the hook.
- **Photo and visual platform:** strong first slide or frame; saves and shares usually signal value; stories suit behind-the-scenes and time-sensitive items.
- **Short text platform:** sharp takes, fast feedback, good for testing ideas; keep outbound links out of the first post if reach matters (test it).
- **Anywhere:** early engagement, genuine replies and steady cadence help; engagement bait and cross-posted identical content usually do not.
Lengths, posting times and counts are for the client to confirm; this skill gives none as facts.

## Measurement

Pick one goal per period (growth, engagement, traffic, conversion) and 2 or 3 metrics that match it: reach and follows for growth; comments, saves, shares for engagement; clicks and sign-ups for traffic and conversion. Review weekly after enough time has passed for the posts to settle. Rank posts into top, middle and bottom thirds, study the top and bottom, reuse winners (refreshed, after a gap), and drop formats that stay at the bottom. Compare against the account's own average, not other accounts. Fill the review block of the calendar template.

## Output rules

- End with a concrete next step (approve the calendar, pick a hook, send the source for repurposing).
- Label assumptions and placeholders; do not invent customer proof, numbers or results.
- Save as a NEW Google Doc or Sheet if a connection exists, otherwise reply in the conversation; never overwrite an existing file.
- Never post, schedule, send, reply publicly or spend money without the user's explicit go-ahead.
- Offer a visual (calendar grid, content map) via the `visual-thinking` skill if installed, otherwise a plain-text table.

## Soft suggestions (use if installed, otherwise do the step in place)

- A brand-voice skill for tone before drafting at volume.
- A copywriting skill for sales or launch posts that need deeper persuasion.
- A search-content skill when topics should follow keyword demand.
- A design or video skill for carousels, thumbnails and scripts; otherwise hand over a plain brief (platform, format, text per slide or shot, tone).

## When not to use

- Defining the brand voice itself: use a brand-voice skill first.
- Long sales pages, ads or email sequences: use a copywriting or email skill.
- Paid social buying, budgets or targeting: use an advertising skill.
- Full go-to-market or positioning decisions: use a strategy skill.

## Quality bar

- Every opening passes the swap test: if the topic can be replaced and the hook still works, rewrite it.
- Every piece is native to its platform, not copied.
- The value-to-ask mix is shown for the calendar.
- Cadence fits the hours the client really has.
- Claims, numbers and testimonials are real or marked as placeholders.
- No engagement bait, no filler phrases, no padding.
- The plan ends with a measurable goal and a review date.
