# Repurposing map

One idea becomes many pieces. The pieces are rebuilt for each destination, not resized. Platform columns are reference points; confirm current formats and lengths on each platform.

## Pick the extraction path

| Source | Path | Typical yield (reference point) |
|---|---|---|
| Video, talk, webinar | Pillar-to-micro: clips, quote graphics, text posts, carousels | 8 to 15 moments |
| Podcast | Audio clips, pull quotes, topic threads, summary post | 6 to 10 moments |
| Newsletter or blog | Hub-and-spoke: each point becomes a standalone post | 6 to 8 per 1,000 words |
| Daily work, no source | Document, do not create: capture what happened, then package | Whatever the week produced |
| Thin source (under about 4 moments) | Go deeper on the source first, or merge with another | Not enough to fan out |

## Steps

1. Capture or transcribe the source.
2. Mark moments: thesis, story, surprise, step, contrarian claim, data point, conclusion.
3. Score each: stands alone (yes/no), would stop a scroll (yes/no). Keep only double yes.
4. Rebuild per destination (table below).
5. Write a fresh opening for each (see hook-patterns).
6. Space them over 4 to 6 weeks, strongest early, ask last; link back to the source only where natural.
7. Record the source-to-post map.

## One idea, many shapes (invented example)

Idea: "Batch your customer replies once a day instead of all day." Source: a weekly newsletter for small shop owners.

| Destination | Rebuild |
|---|---|
| Professional network text post | Personal story of the week replies were ignored, what changed, one closing question |
| Visual platform carousel | Five slides: problem, the batching rule, a sample schedule, the result, a save prompt |
| Short vertical video | Face to camera, state the rule in the first seconds, show the inbox, one-line takeaway |
| Short text platform | One sharp sentence of the rule, plus a reply with the schedule |
| Story or ephemeral | Poll: "Do you answer all day or in batches?" then share the result |
| Newsletter | Full piece; link only from a few posts |

## Quality gate

- Standalone: a reader who never saw the source still gets full value.
- Native: format, length and call to action fit the platform.
- Fresh: different angle, example or entry point on each platform.
- Mixed: vary styles; avoid twelve tips in a row.
- Honest: no invented numbers or proof.

## Idea validation (cheap test first)

Post the core idea as a very short post. If it clearly beats the account's average, expand it (post, carousel, video, newsletter topic). If it falls flat, do not invest more.

## Evergreen library

After 3 months of history, keep a list of the top-third posts. Re-queue them after at least several months, refreshed (new hook, new example, updated facts). Drop any that underperform on the second run. Never recycle time-sensitive posts without rewriting. The library supplements new work; it is not the strategy.

## Platform adaptation cheat sheet (verify, then use)

| Platform type | Tone | Strong formats | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short vertical video | Raw, quick, personal | Vertical video | Cut preamble, show the face, hook at once |
| Professional network | Opinionated, story-led | Text, document carousel, native video | Lead with a first-person angle |
| Visual platform | Visual, relatable | Carousel, short video, story | Strong first frame, save-worthy slide |
| Short text platform | Punchy, conversational | Single post, thread | Sharpest version of the idea first |
