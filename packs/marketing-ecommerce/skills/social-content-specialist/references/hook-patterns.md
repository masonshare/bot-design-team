# Hook patterns and engagement mechanics

A hook is the packaging that earns the next few seconds (video) or the first line (text). It is not the message. It must honestly lead into the content; a broken promise costs trust and retention.

## Patterns (write several, pick one)

Examples below are invented.

| Pattern | Shape | Invented example |
|---|---|---|
| Specific result | Outcome plus a concrete constraint | "A two-person bakery cut waste by half with one change to Friday's order." |
| Counterintuitive claim | Contradict a common belief, then promise the reason | "More posts did not grow this shop. Fewer, longer ones did." |
| Mistake reveal | Own a failure, tease the lesson | "I overpriced my first course for a year. Here is what I missed." |
| Question with a stake | Question the reader privately cares about | "Why do your best customers never leave reviews?" |
| Before and after | Two states, one change | "Before: 30 unread DMs. After: one saved reply template." |
| Pattern interrupt | Unexpected image, sound or first word | Open on the finished result, then rewind. |
| Named audience | Call out the exact reader | "If you run a one-person studio and hate invoicing, read this." |
| Open loop list | Number plus a hidden item | "Four packing mistakes. The third surprised our whole team." |
| Myth versus fact | State the myth, flip it | "Myth: posting at 9am wins. Fact: posting when your buyers are idle wins." |

## Swap test

If you can change the topic and the hook still works, it is generic. Add a concrete detail, a number you can defend, a named audience or a real constraint.

## Arc

Hook, then story or evidence that keeps referring back to the hook, then takeaway with one next step. Video: hook in the first seconds, takeaway in the last few. Text: hook before the fold, takeaway as the closing line.

## Why people share (checklist)

- Social currency: passing it on makes the sharer look informed.
- Triggers: it links to something people meet often (a weekday, a habit, a season).
- Emotion: high-energy feelings (awe, amusement, justified anger, excitement) travel; low-energy ones (calm satisfaction, sadness) travel less. Use anger sparingly and aim it at a problem, not a person.
- Public: easy to see, join or copy.
- Practical value: concise, immediately usable, worth saving.
- Story: the message rides inside a narrative.
Most strong posts hit two or three, not all six.

## Pre-publish score

Usefulness, inspiration and empathy, each 1 to 5. Any score under 3: revise. Any zero: the post is not ready.

## Repeatable formats

A format is a reusable structure (walk-and-talk list, product teardown, two characters debating, quick street question, screen walkthrough) that works for many topics. Choose one to three that fit the client's time, equipment and personality, and master one before adding another. Study 5 to 10 strong, average and weak examples of a format (top, middle, bottom) to see what separates them, and copy the effect on the viewer rather than the surface tactic.

## Engagement mechanics

- Ask a question only when you will read and answer the replies.
- Give the first comments a reason to exist: a specific prompt, not "thoughts?".
- Use real scarcity only (a real deadline, a real cap); constant urgency destroys it.
- Show real proof (permissioned customer words, real results); never invent it.
- Banned crutches: "drop an emoji if you agree", "most people do not know this", "let that sink in", "read that again", stock openers such as "in today's world".

## Hook workshop in 5 minutes

1. Write the one-sentence takeaway.
2. Write 8 openings, at least 4 different patterns.
3. Delete any that fail the swap test.
4. Read the survivors aloud; pick the one you would stop for.
5. Keep the runner-up as the variant.
