# Frameworks: behavioural and psychological tactics

Each entry: what it is, when to use, one invented example line. Use them honestly: they shape how information is received, they do not replace facts. Invented examples; check any rule-of-thumb figure against your own market.

## Judgement biases

**12. Anchoring**
What: the first number in the room pulls later numbers toward it. A well-supported high (or low) opening shifts the whole range.
Use: when you open, and when the other side opens with an extreme figure (reject it explicitly and re-anchor with reasons).
Example: "Based on three comparable projects I charge 18,500; here is how it breaks down."

**13. Framing**
What: the same offer feels different as a gain or a loss, as a share of a total or an absolute figure. Choose the frame that fits your case and name the frame they are using.
Use: when presenting your offer or when they present theirs.
Example: "That is less than the cost of one lost week of production, not a 12 percent price rise."

**14. Loss aversion and inertia**
What: people dislike losing more than they like gaining the same thing, and tend to keep the current state and overvalue what they own. Offers feel safer when they show what is protected.
Use: when the other side resists change, or when you are asking them to give up something they hold.
Example: "If you stay with the old tool, the manual rework continues at about 10 hours a week; here is how the switch removes that."

## Influence

**15. Reciprocity**
What: a real favour or a small first concession creates a pull to return it. It must be genuine and proportionate.
Use: to start movement; give first on a point that costs you little.
Example: "I will include the extra report at no charge; could we then settle the delivery date today?"

**16. Commitment**
What: people stay consistent with positions they state or write. Ask for small, clear agreements early, in the other side's own words.
Use: to secure agreed principles before detailed terms.
Example: "You said predictable monthly costs matter most; can I build the proposal around that?"

**17. Credibility and rapport**
What: people give more to those they trust, like, or see as similar or expert. Show relevant evidence, third-party results and shared goals without inflating them.
Use: first meetings, cold or low-trust relationships.
Example: "Two similar shops moved to this setup last year; I can share how it went for them with their permission."

**18. Genuine scarcity**
What: a real limit (capacity, time, supply) raises perceived value. Only use one that is true; an invented limit is deceit and breaks the ethics line.
Use: when you truly have limited slots or a real alternative.
Example: "I have capacity for one more client this quarter and hold the slot until Thursday."

## Conversation tactics

**19. Echo and label**
What: repeat the last few words of what they said as a question to draw out more; name the emotion you hear ("it sounds like...") to lower tension. Keep the voice slow and low.
Use: when they hold back, or when feeling runs high.
Example: "It sounds like the last supplier let you down on delivery dates." / "The dates are what worry you?"

**20. Calibrated questions**
What: open questions starting "how" or "what" that make them work on the problem without being pushed. Avoid "why" (feels like blame) and yes/no questions.
Use: to refuse politely, probe constraints, or turn a demand into a shared problem.
Example: "How am I supposed to deliver that at this budget?"

**21. Pre-empting objections**
What: say the worst things they might think about you or the deal before they do. It disarms them and shows you understood.
Use: at the start of a sensitive request or after a past failure.
Example: "You may feel I am pushing for more after already agreeing a rate, and I understand."

**22. Confirming real agreement**
What: a summary that makes them answer "that is right" shows true understanding; a polite "yes" or "you are right" can mean disengaged. Confirm key points three times in different forms (rephrase, restate, ask who will do it by when) before closing.
Use: before signing, and any time a "yes" seems too easy.
Example: "So the order is 400 units, delivered in two parts, and your team confirms the dock slot; have I got that?"
