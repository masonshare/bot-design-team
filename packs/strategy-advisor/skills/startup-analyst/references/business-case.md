# Business case workflow

Use when the user must decide whether and how to proceed, or needs a document for a board, partner or investor. Every figure is tagged `given`, `sourced` or `assumption`. Do not invent market data or traction.

## Step 1. Set scope and audience

Ask once, with defaults:
1. Decision being made, and who decides. (default: go or no-go on the main plan)
2. Audience: internal, board, investor, partner. (default: internal)
3. Length: one page, short (3 to 5 pages), or full. (default: short)
4. What evidence exists: interviews, pilots, usage, signed intent, financials. (default: none)

If a market estimate, projection or metrics already exist, reuse them. If not, run the market workflow or the financial workflow first, or include a rough version labelled as such.

## Step 2. Frame the problem

- Who has it, how many (tagged), how often it occurs.
- Current workaround and what it costs them: hours, money, errors, lost deals.
- Evidence strength: strong (paid behaviour), medium (stated intent), weak (opinion). Say which.
- Why it is unsolved: cost, technology, regulation, attention.

## Step 3. Generate options

At least three, always including:
- **Do nothing** (cost of delay stated).
- **Minimal variant** (smallest test that gives real evidence).
- **Full plan**.

Optionally: partner, buy versus build, or a different segment first. For each option record investment, time to first revenue, expected benefit per year, team needed, main risk, reversibility.

## Step 4. Compare with ROI

Per option and per scenario (conservative, base, optimistic):

| Measure | How |
|---|---|
| Total investment over the horizon | One-off plus running cost |
| Annual benefit | Revenue gained or cost saved |
| Payback time | Months until cumulative benefit covers investment |
| Simple return | (cumulative benefit minus investment) divided by investment |
| Break-even point | Customers or volume needed |

Use a discount rate only if the user wants it, and state it as an assumption. Keep the maths visible so the reader can change an input.

## Step 5. Score non-financial factors

Rate each option low, medium or high on: strategic fit, execution difficulty, learning value, regulatory or reputation risk. Short note per rating. Do not hide a weak financial case behind soft factors.

## Step 6. Risks

Top five. For each: description, likelihood, impact, early warning sign, mitigation, and whether it is a stop trigger.

## Step 7. Recommendation

- The chosen option and the reason it beats the others.
- What must be true (3 to 5 conditions), each with a way to test it.
- First milestone, date or time frame, and owner.
- The condition that would reverse the decision.
- What is deliberately not being done.

## Step 8. Sections for an investor version

Add, in this order: summary; problem; solution; market (from the market workflow); competition; business model and go-to-market; projections (from the financial workflow); team and open roles; traction and milestones; risks; funding ask and use of funds with the milestones it buys. Each section should be half a page or less in a short version.

Funding ask: state the amount as an assumption tied to a runway target and the milestone set, not as a round number chosen by habit.

## Layout of the output

```
# Business case: [initiative]
Decision and recommendation (3 lines)
Problem and evidence
Options table
ROI table by scenario
Risks
Recommendation, conditions, milestone
Assumptions table and gaps
Next step this week
```

## Quality checks

- Do-nothing is present and costed.
- Benefits are not counted twice across options.
- Costs include people time, not only cash.
- The base case is not the optimistic case renamed.
- A reader can find the three assumptions that matter most in under a minute.
- The document would still make sense to someone who has not heard the pitch.

## Common mistakes

- Presenting one option dressed up as three.
- Using total market size as the revenue forecast.
- Leaving out the time the founders will spend.
- Treating interest as demand (unpaid sign-ups are weak evidence).
- A recommendation with no stop condition.
