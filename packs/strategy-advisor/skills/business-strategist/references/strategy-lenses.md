# Strategy lenses (compact)

Use these for Cartographer and Architect modes. Each lens: what it asks, how to apply it, what evidence to cite. All ratings need evidence from the user or are labelled assumptions.

## Five Forces (industry structure)

Question: why is profit high or low in this industry, and who captures it?

| Force | Rate High when | Typical evidence to ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry among competitors | many similar players, slow growth, high fixed costs, low switching cost | number of players, price moves, discounting |
| Threat of new entrants | low capital need, no scale or brand advantage, easy distribution | time and money to launch a rival |
| Substitutes | other ways to get the same job done cheaper or easier | what customers did before, spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing |
| Buyer power | few large buyers, low switching cost, price-transparent | share of revenue from top customers |
| Supplier power | few suppliers, critical inputs, suppliers could integrate forward | dependence on one platform or vendor |

Output: one row per force with High, Medium or Low, one line of evidence, and the "so what" for the user's strategy. Overall: is the industry structurally attractive, or attractive only for a particular position?

## Durable-advantage sources

Ask which of these the business owns today, could build, or lacks. An advantage must both improve the business's economics and be hard to copy.

| Source | Test question |
|---|---|
| Scale economies | Does unit cost fall as volume rises, and would a smaller rival be stuck at higher cost? |
| Network effects | Does each added user make the product more valuable to the others? |
| Switching costs | What would a customer lose, pay or redo to leave? |
| Brand | Will customers pay more or choose faster for this name? |
| Cornered resource | Does the business control a talent, licence, dataset or contract that rivals cannot get? |
| Counter-positioning | Does the business use a model the leader would hurt itself by copying? |
| Process power | Does an embedded way of working give lower cost or higher quality that is slow to replicate? |

Rule: if the answer is "none yet", state it, then recommend which one to build first given the stage.

## Generic strategies and "stuck in the middle"

- Cost leadership: lowest delivered cost, price at or slightly below the market. Needs scale, tight operations.
- Differentiation: unique value customers pay extra for. Needs a real, defensible difference.
- Focus: serve one segment better than broad players, via cost or differentiation.
- Warning sign of drift: premium cost structure with discount pricing, or broad claims with no clear buyer. Name the missing choice.

## Value chain (coherence check)

List the activities from inbound inputs through production, delivery, sales, service, and support functions (technology, people, finance). For the chosen strategy ask: which two or three activities must be exceptional, are they today, and are they reinforcing each other? Gaps in a critical activity are the real risk of a strategy, more than the strategy statement itself.

## Strategy canvas and eliminate, reduce, raise, create

1. List the 6 to 10 factors customers compare in this industry (price, speed, setup effort, support, range, and similar).
2. Score the leading competitors and the user 1 to 5 on each; draw a line per player (text table if no chart tool).
3. If all lines look alike, the market is crowded on shared factors.
4. Fill the grid: which factors to eliminate (taken for granted but not valued), reduce (over-served), raise (under-served), create (nobody offers).
5. A good new position has a clear focus, a few factors raised, and a tagline-length message. If it needs all four actions on every factor, it is not focused.

## Growth-direction matrix

| | Existing market | New market |
|---|---|---|
| Existing product | Penetration: sell more of the same to the same buyers (lowest risk) | Market development: new segment, region or channel |
| New product | Product development: new offer for current customers | Diversification: new offer and new market (highest risk) |

Use it to compare options by risk. Penetration is usually tried first; the further from the current cell, the more evidence and cash should be required. Cross-check with economics and capabilities.

## SWOT and TOWS

SWOT lists internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats; alone it is a list, not a strategy. Convert to TOWS pairs:
- Strengths with opportunities: use strengths to grab opportunities (growth moves).
- Strengths with threats: use strengths to blunt threats (defensive moves).
- Weaknesses with opportunities: fix a weakness to unlock an opportunity (build moves).
- Weaknesses with threats: avoid or exit (retreat moves).
Keep each cell to evidence-backed items, then pick at most three moves and put them into the options table.

## Positioning statement (short form)

"For [target buyer] who [problem], [offer] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], it [proof of difference]." Test it by asking whether a competitor could truthfully say the same sentence; if so, it is not a position.
