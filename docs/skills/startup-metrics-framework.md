# startup-metrics-framework

## What it does

Chooses, calculates and reads the right 5 to 7 startup metrics for a business model and stage (recurring revenue, CAC, LTV, payback, retention, burn multiple, marketplace and consumer engagement), then sets up a reporting rhythm. Body in English, description bilingual.

Startups track the wrong or too many numbers. The skill picks few, matched to the stage, puts the formula next to the number, and treats benchmarks as reference points to check against the user's own market. The earlier playbook files are merged into a single SKILL.md. Reference points: LTV to CAC above about 3; CAC payback under 12 months is very good, 12 to 18 is good, 18 to 24 is borderline, over 24 is worrying; burn multiple under 1 excellent, 1 to 2 acceptable, over 2 poor; net dollar retention above 120 percent is best in class; keep 12 to 18 months of runway. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market; the skill asks for your own figures.

Source: [`packs/strategy-advisor/skills/startup-metrics-framework/SKILL.md`](../../packs/strategy-advisor/skills/startup-metrics-framework/SKILL.md)

## When to reach for it

The user asks about key metrics, CAC and LTV, unit economics, burn multiple, or which numbers to show investors. For a full forecast use `startup-financial-modeling`; for shop-level sales numbers use `ecommerce-kpi-analytics`.

## Common questions

**Why only 5 to 7 metrics?** More than that hides the ones that matter at your stage.

**Are the benchmarks facts?** No. They are common reference points; check them against your sector.

**Does it work outside software?** Partly. The formulas hold, but the benchmarks need replacing.

## It's working if

- It asks for the business model and stage before choosing metrics.
- Each metric comes with its formula and a labelled reference point.
- It ends with a reporting rhythm.
