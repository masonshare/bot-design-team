# ecommerce-promotion-design

## What it does

Designs a consumer promotion for an online shop: three questions first, then one of six mechanisms (price cut, bundle, gift with purchase, voucher, lucky draw, loyalty), a campaign calendar, and a profit calculation that subtracts the post-promotion dip. It outputs a promotion brief and a 3-day review.

Promotions go wrong by cutting too deep, too often, and ignoring the sales that disappear afterwards. The skill makes the dip part of the maths. Worked examples use assumed numbers and say so. Reference points: a cut under about 10 percent rarely moves buyers, and over about 30 percent often hurts margin; no more than about 2 weeks of continuous discount on one SKU; bundle price 10 to 20 percent below buying separately; a dip under 30 percent of the extra sales is acceptable; big campaigns need 2 to 3 times normal stock, prepared 2 to 4 weeks ahead. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market; the skill asks for your own figures. It does not create vouchers or register for platform campaigns.

## When to reach for it

An owner asks how to design a promotion, whether to join a platform flash sale, what voucher percentage to use, how to know a promotion made money, or what to do for a big sale day. For sales diagnosis use `ecommerce-kpi-analytics`.

## Common questions

**Why does it ask three questions first?** The goal, the customers and the budget decide the mechanism; without them any mechanism is a guess.

**Are the example numbers real?** No. They are assumptions and are labelled as such. Use your own.

**Does it set up the voucher?** No. It writes the plan; you set it up on the platform.

## It's working if

- It asks its three questions before choosing a mechanism.
- The profit calculation includes the post-promotion dip.
- The brief ends with a review step within 3 days of the campaign.
