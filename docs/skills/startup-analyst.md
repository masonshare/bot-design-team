# startup-analyst

## What it does

An early-stage startup analyst for pre-seed to Series A: it sizes the market (top-down and bottom-up, TAM/SAM/SOM), maps competitors, and writes a business case (problem, options, ROI, recommendation), with every number labelled as an assumption. It always opens with the customer problem and always includes a top-down view. Body in English, description bilingual.

Founders and early investors need a believable market size, an honest competitive picture and a business case that supports a decision. The skill never invents market figures; examples are invented (a booking tool for dog groomers) and labelled. Detailed staffing, organisation and fundraising content from the earlier version was dropped. The earlier version looked like it came from a community skill collection with an unverified licence, so this page records that this is a from-scratch rewrite, not a redistribution. Reference points: bottom-up and top-down estimates differing by about 2 times means hunt for the assumption causing it; at least 3 business-case options including "do nothing" and a minimal version; 4 to 6 competitor comparison criteria; fundraising buffer of 6 months; an advantage lasting 12 to 24 months is the window for asking whether a bigger rival can copy it. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market; the skill asks for your own figures.

Source: [`packs/strategy-advisor/skills/startup-analyst/SKILL.md`](../../packs/strategy-advisor/skills/startup-analyst/SKILL.md)

## When to reach for it

Someone asks how big the market is, for TAM SAM SOM, who the competitors are, for a business case, whether something is worth building, or for investor-ready analysis. For a full financial model use `startup-financial-modeling`; for metrics use `startup-metrics-framework`; for competitor battlecards and positioning use `market-researcher`, which overlaps on market sizing.

## Common questions

**Where do the market numbers come from?** From you or a search tool; anything else is a labelled assumption. It never invents market data.

**Why does it always show a top-down estimate?** Without one the bottom-up number has nothing to be checked against.

**How is it different from `market-researcher`?** That skill is broader on competitors and positioning; this one is aimed at investors and ends in a business case.

## It's working if

- The answer opens with the customer problem.
- Both top-down and bottom-up sizing are shown, and a gap over about 2 times is explained.
- The business case has at least 3 options, including doing nothing.
