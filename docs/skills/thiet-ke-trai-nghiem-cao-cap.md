# thiet-ke-trai-nghiem-cao-cap

## What it does

Guides a founder, brand strategist or customer-experience designer to design a premium experience at the right tier for B2B, B2C, hospitality, food and beverage or similar. A five-phase method: diagnose, design, operationalise, culturalise, measure, built on the idea that premium means what the customer lacks in ordinary life, delivered at the right moment beyond expectation. Body in Vietnamese, description bilingual. Three small asset files hold a tier diagnostic, phase checklists and a measurement dashboard.

Many businesses invest in appearance (a showroom, packaging) and customers still do not feel premium. The skill moves from diagnosing what the customer lacks to concrete moments, operations, culture and measurement at the right tier. Output goes to a Google Doc if connected; a printable export is optional. Known weak spot from testing: heavy jargon and a mention of asset files the user cannot see. Reference points: keep 3 to 5 lacks and focus on 1 to 2; a feasibility plus memorability score of 8 or more means do now, 7 consider, 5 to 6 keep for later, under 5 drop; 1 to 2 moments (peak-end rule); at least 3 of 5 senses; at least 3 customer interviews; review every 6 months; retest the script with about 5 customers (over 70 percent still delighted is fine, under 40 percent escalate). Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market; the skill asks for your own figures.

## When to reach for it

Someone asks to design a premium or luxury experience, audit the customer experience, says customers do not feel it is premium, or asks about a premium showroom or touchpoints. For mapping the whole customer journey use `journey-mapper`; for brand voice use `brand-architect`.

## Common questions

**What does "premium" mean here?** What the customer lacks in ordinary life, delivered at the right moment beyond expectation, at the tier of customer you serve.

**Do I need to interview customers?** At least 3 for the diagnosis; without them the diagnosis is a guess and is labelled one.

**Where do the examples come from?** All scripts and examples are invented.

## It's working if

- The diagnosis names what the customer lacks, checked against at least 3 customers or labelled as a guess.
- Only 1 to 2 key moments are designed, with senses and operations behind them.
- A measurement plan and a 6-month review date exist.
