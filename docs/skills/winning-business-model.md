# winning-business-model

## What it does

Builds or stress-tests a business model for one venture around four questions (WHO, WHAT, HOW, HOW MUCH) and one value equation, then delivers a verdict (Go, Go to test, Refine, No-Go) with a one-page canvas and a decision memo of at most 200 words. The default is automatic mode: the agent drafts everything and the user reacts at two decision points; say "coach mode" for step-by-step questions. Body in Vietnamese, description bilingual.

Founders often have a product and a few loose numbers but no model that links the customer, the value, the delivery and the money. The skill makes the four answers check each other (five tests of the value equation and a consistency matrix) and labels the evidence on every important line. Any key assumption stays marked as an assumption, so with only guessed numbers an early-stage idea is capped: at most "Go to test", and a contradiction means Refine. "Go to test" requires three things: the test conditions (what to test, with whom, a reference window of 2 to 6 weeks, a spending cap the user sets), the success metrics written before the test, and stop thresholds that may not be loosened after seeing results; the memo has an "after the test" line. Reference points: exactly 1 segment chosen out of 3 to 6 scored; more than about 40 percent "very disappointed if it disappeared" as a fit signal; LTV to CAC under 1 is a loss, 1 to under 3 thin, 3 or more healthy (low-repeat or long-cycle businesses differ); benefit worth about 3 times the price makes the decision easy; 2 decision points. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market or channel; the skill asks for your own figures. Trade-off: the verdict is held at Refine until there are real numbers, and most output carries an [assumption] tag in automatic mode.

## When to reach for it

Someone asks to build a business model, check whether a model is winning, check product-market fit, work out LTV and CAC, or review the value proposition and pricing end to end for one venture. For a full financial model use `startup-financial-modeling`; for prices use `pricing-strategist`; for market sizing use `startup-analyst`.

## Common questions

**Why is my verdict stuck at Refine or Go to test?** Because the key numbers are still assumptions. Bring real numbers to move it, or run the test the memo describes.

**What are the two decision points?** Choosing the one segment, and approving the value equation; everything else is drafted for you to react to.

**Can I walk through it step by step?** Yes, say "coach mode".

## It's working if

- The model has exactly one chosen segment and a value equation, and each key line is labelled by evidence level.
- The verdict follows the cap rules and, for Go to test, includes conditions, metrics written first and stop thresholds.
- A one-page canvas and a memo of at most 200 words are delivered.
