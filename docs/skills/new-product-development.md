# new-product-development

## What it does

Guides a founder or small team through developing a new physical product (toys, food and beverage, cosmetics, consumer goods, gift sets) from idea to concept, formula or specification, packaging and a multi-channel launch (marketplaces plus an own website). It runs six phases and 22 steps, each with a self-check, outputs and a decision gate per phase, and three approval gates. Body in Vietnamese, description bilingual. A helper file lists checklists by product type, and `evals/evals.json` holds three generic test cases for later use.

Founders and small teams often stop at a concept on paper or jump straight into manufacturing. The gates make it possible to stop early instead of stopping with stock on the shelf. The first reply always shows the six-phase map, including what packaging and launch will need, because a test run only did phases 1 and 2 and left the rest as headings; special situations cover joining midway and having only a competitor's product photos. The process comes from the owner's own operating procedure. Reference points: a shortlist of at most 3 ideas; 5 to 10 people to test the concept; 2 to 3 rounds of sample revisions; at most 2 "fail" rows in the five-lens check, with Finance and User never failing; a compressed 4-week schedule that is only realistic with ready-made templates. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market or channel; the skill asks for your own figures. The evals file has three cases with invented products, 5 "must" items each: a full run from an idea, resuming at packaging, and having only competitor photos.

## When to reach for it

Someone asks to develop a new product, go from idea to launch, run the full process, write a product concept, or resume at the packaging phase. It can feed the pack's brand, copy and image skills; for the launch prices use `pricing-strategist` and for the business model use `winning-business-model`. Links are soft.

## Common questions

**Can I start in the middle?** Yes. It asks where you are and what exists, and starts at that phase.

**What are the three gates?** Decision points where the approval pack is reviewed; you can stop there instead of continuing.

**What is `evals/evals.json` for?** Three reusable test cases for checking the skill later; it is not loaded when the skill runs.

## It's working if

- The first reply shows all six phases with a one-line output each, including what packaging and launch need.
- The current phase and the next decision gate are stated.
- Assumptions are listed with what the process still needs from you.
