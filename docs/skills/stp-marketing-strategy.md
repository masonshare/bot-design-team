# stp-marketing-strategy

## What it does

Runs Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning through the Jobs To Be Done lens: segment by the job customers hire a product for (functional, emotional, social, meaning), score the segments and pick an under-served one, then write a positioning statement and a rough 4P mix. It starts with a context step and ends with an STP report. Body in Vietnamese, description bilingual.

Many small businesses market in scattered ways because they have not decided who the customer is and why they would choose the shop. Using the job as the unit keeps segments, target and positioning connected instead of three separate exercises. The job comes before demographics; geography, demographics, psychology and behaviour stay as descriptors for reaching people. The trap of confusing a job with a solution is flagged at the top of step 1. Step 0 reads `brand-profile.md` and asks only for what is missing. Reference points: 3 to 6 job segments, pick 1 to 2; a 7-criteria scorecard of 1 to 5 each, total 35 (28 and above is strong, 21 to 27 is worth considering); name 5 real customers you could reach within 30 days; validate with 5 to 10 customer interviews. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market or brand; the skill asks for your own figures. Trade-off: without real customer data the scores are the user's estimates, clearly labelled, so the user can still finish all three steps at lower accuracy.

## When to reach for it

Someone asks for STP, JTBD or jobs to be done, to segment the market, to find the target customer, to position, to build a customer persona, or just to "analyse my market". Modes are keywords (express, guided, deep). A diagram is only an optional offer via a diagramming skill, with a table fallback. For brand voice use `brand-architect`; for market sizing and competitors use `market-researcher`.

## Common questions

**Why jobs instead of demographics?** Two people with the same age and income can hire the product for different jobs; the job predicts the choice better, and demographics then describe how to reach them.

**What if I have no customer data?** It still runs; scores become labelled estimates and it tells you which interviews would firm them up.

**Does it need the other skills?** No. Other skills are soft suggestions and it works in place when they are not installed.

## It's working if

- Segments are defined by jobs, not by demographics alone.
- Each candidate is scored on the 7 criteria and one or two are chosen, with the reason.
- The report ends with a positioning statement and a rough 4P mix.
