# pitch-bod-bos-dt

## What it does

Builds a board pitch for a strategic idea from audience map to rehearsal: it pairs a strategy canvas, ERRC, four actions, six paths and non-customer tiers with Design Thinking (empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test), then produces a one-page memo, a deck storyline, rehearsal and a retro. Body in Vietnamese, description bilingual. The "bos" in the name is kept on purpose.

Good strategy ideas get rejected by boards for lack of evidence about real people and a short market argument. The skill ties understanding people who are not yet customers to value-strategy tools. Nine separate templates of the earlier version were folded into SKILL.md and three files (a non-customer tiers worksheet, a pitch storyline template, a board objection bank); all examples are invented. Known weak spot: it runs a 10-step process and asks many inputs before the first draft. Reference points: 5 to 7 factors on the canvas; 10 to 20 interviews (express 5 to 8); about 60 percent of weight on the "refuse" non-customer tier; slogan under 12 words; about half of the meeting for questions and answers; 3 to 5 problem-definition sentences; meet 2 to 3 people beforehand for about 30 minutes. Every number in this skill is a labelled reference point, not a fact about your market; the skill asks for your own figures.

## When to reach for it

Someone says "pitch the board", "make a board deck", "the board rejected my idea", or is preparing a board meeting or persuading a board to invest. For general speaking skills use `thuyet-trinh-winston` when it is installed, which is a real boundary between the two; for negotiating terms use `elite-negotiator`.

## Common questions

**Why so many steps?** A board decides on evidence about people and on the argument, so the process builds both; an express mode uses fewer interviews.

**Do I need interview data?** It is better with it. Without it, it labels estimates and says which interviews to run.

**Which deliverables come out?** A one-page memo, a deck storyline, a question-and-answer plan and a retro.

## It's working if

- The pitch has a canvas, ERRC and non-customer tiers backed by evidence or labelled estimates.
- A one-page memo and a deck storyline exist, with a slogan under 12 words.
- The board's likely objections are answered in a prepared question-and-answer plan.
